# Billing and Usage

**Last verified:** 2026-10-27 9:29am

WebNesting uses simple, pay-for-what-you-use pricing. You are only charged for the features and resources your site actually uses. This page explains how billing works, what things cost, and how to keep track of your spending.

---

## Understanding Your Bill

Your WebNesting bill is made up of two types of charges:

1. **Usage-based charges** -- These depend on how much of something you use (like the number of pages on your site or how much file storage you need).
2. **Module fees** -- These are flat monthly charges for add-on features you have enabled (like Articles, Events, or E-Commerce).

You are billed once a month. Each bill covers the previous month's usage and shows a detailed breakdown of every charge.

---

## What Is Included for Free

Every WebNesting site comes with a few things at no charge:

- **Your first 5 pages** -- You can have up to 5 pages on your site before any page charges apply. This includes any type of page (regular pages, article pages, event pages, etc.). Turning on the Articles, Events, or Store module adds 10 more free pages to that site for as long as the module stays on -- so a site with Store gets 15 free pages, and 25 with Store and Articles both on.
- **Your first 2 GB of storage** -- Everything we store for you (your own uploads, helpdesk attachments, and files visitors upload through your forms) is measured together across your whole workspace, and the first 2 GB is free.

These free allowances apply automatically. You do not need to do anything to activate them. If you stay within these limits, you will not see those items on your bill at all.

## Usage-Based Pricing

Usage-based charges grow or shrink depending on your actual use. Here is what is tracked and how much it costs.

### Pages

**$0.50 per page per month** (first 5 pages free, +10 more per page module turned on)

Every page on your site counts toward your page total. This includes:
- Regular pages (Home, About, Contact, etc.)
- Article/blog post pages
- Event pages
- Product pages
- Any other page type

System pages (the built-in pages every site ships with, like the 404 page) are always free and do not count toward your total.

Your free page allowance isn't fixed at 5 -- turning on the Articles, Events, or Store module gives that site 10 more free pages for as long as the module stays on, on top of the 5 every site gets. A site with just Store turned on has 15 free pages; with Store and Articles both on, 25.

**Example:** If your site has 15 pages and no page modules (Articles, Events, or Store) turned on, the first 5 are free, and you pay for the remaining 10. That is 10 x $0.50 = $5.00 per month for pages. Turn on the Store module and those same 15 pages are all free, since Store's 10 extra free pages bring your allowance up to 15.

> **Tip:** You can see your current page count in your dashboard. If you are looking to reduce costs, consider removing pages you no longer need.

### Storage

**$1 per GB per month** (first 2 GB free)

Storage is one meter for your whole workspace. It covers everything we store for you: your own uploads (images, videos, documents), helpdesk ticket attachments, and files visitors upload through your forms. You are billed once for storage no matter how many sites you have.

**Example:** If you are storing 3 GB of files in total, the first 2 GB are free, and you pay for 1 GB. That is 1 x $1 = $1.00 per month for storage.

Files belonging to a product you've turned off -- such as the attachments on your Helpdesk tickets -- still count toward storage while they're kept, and stop counting once they're deleted.

> **Tip:** You can see and manage every stored file from the File Manager (click **Files** in the top bar) -- helpdesk attachments live in the managed **Helpdesk** folder and visitor uploads in **Form Uploads**. Deleting files you no longer need lowers your bill immediately.

### Website bandwidth

**$0.20 per GB** (first 10 GB free each month)

Website bandwidth is the data your public websites send to your visitors: your pages, pictures, videos and files. Each time a visitor loads or watches something, the bytes sent are added to your workspace's total. Videos send the most. All your websites count together as one meter, so you are billed once no matter how many sites you have. The line is named **Website Bandwidth** on the **Usage** page and on your invoice.

**Example:** If your sites send 30 GB in a month, the first 10 GB are free and you pay for 20 GB. That is 20 x $0.20 = $4.00 for the month.

- **It is billed now.** WebNesting counts your website bandwidth, shows it on the **Usage** page and the **Cost calculator**, and adds it to your bill. Your first 10 GB each month are free.
- **Your 10 free GB build up hour by hour**, like your other free allowances. Website bandwidth has no product to switch on, so the free GB build up all month, every month. A full month always gives you 10 GB. A workspace created partway through a month gets a share of the 10 GB, based on the hours it existed. The **Usage** page row says the exact free amount.
- **What is not counted.** Visits to your WebNesting dashboard and the website builder, and API calls (those are **API Data Transfer**, below). A request that fails, such as a page not found, is not counted. If a visitor stops a video partway, only about the part that was sent is counted.
- **It updates once a day.** The **Usage** page row says it is "Updated daily" and shows the date the count runs through. The newest hours can take a day to appear.
- **A bill with a bandwidth charge can come a little late.** Before an invoice with a bandwidth charge is final, WebNesting checks the count against the network provider's own figures. If that check takes longer, the invoice is sent later than the 1st.

**See where it went.** On the **Usage** page, click the **Website Bandwidth** row. It opens to your free amount, the GB each website sent (bytes that belong to no site show as **Workspace media library**), and **Files that sent the most**: your top 5 files, each with a small picture, its type and where it is. Each site's total includes its pages. Open the line on your invoice, too: **Where it was sent** lists the GB for each site on each day.

**Ways to lower it:**

- **Put long videos on YouTube or Vimeo** and paste the link into the **Video** block. An embedded video costs nothing in website bandwidth.
- Look at **Files that sent the most**. If a video is at the top, use a shorter or smaller version of it.
- Remember that a visitor loads a video only when they press play. A video no one watches sends almost nothing.

WebNesting emails the workspace owner as the charge grows. See [Spending Alerts](#spending-alerts). When you upload a video over 50 MB in **Files**, a note reminds you that videos use website bandwidth.

### People who sign up on your site

**No extra charge.**

When someone creates an account on your site, they become one of your contacts -- the same list your forms, Marketing and Helpdesk already use. They are counted with your contacts (see below), not billed separately, and there is no per-person fee for letting people sign in.

Your team members are not charged either. Everyone you invite to help run your workspace is included.

Modules are add-on features with flat monthly fees. You are only charged for modules you have turned on. Every module also has an exact hourly rate -- the number your bill actually multiplies by for every hour the module is on:

| Module | Rate While On | What It Does |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Articles | $0.006849315068493/hour -- about $5.00/month | Adds a blog/news section to your site |
| Events | $0.006849315068493/hour -- about $5.00/month | Adds event listings with dates and details |
| Forms | $0.013698630136986/hour -- about $10.00/month | Build custom forms for contact, surveys, and registrations |
| E-Commerce (Store) | $0.027397260273973/hour -- about $20.00/month | Adds an online store with products and orders |
| Widget Builder | $0.027397260273973/hour -- about $20.00/month | Lets you create custom interactive elements |
| Hide WebNesting branding | $0.006849315068493/hour -- about $5.00/month | Hides the "Powered by WebNesting" badge on your published site |

You'll notice the hourly rate carries a lot of decimal places. That isn't a display error -- it's the flat monthly price spread evenly across every hour in a year, and it's exactly what you're billed, to the second, for the time the module is on. The "about $X/month" figure next to it is what a full month usually comes out to.

Workspace-level products are billed separately at the workspace level (see [Workspace Products](workspace-products.md)):

| Product | Rate While On | Usage-Based Pricing | What It Does |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|--------------|
| Marketing | $0.013698630136986/hour -- about $10.00/month | $0.70 per 1,000 emails, after about 10,000 free emails a month. Emails sent by your automations count with campaign emails | Email campaigns, contacts, automations |
| Helpdesk | $0.020547945205479/hour -- about $15.00/month | $0.05 per ticket, after about 25 free tickets a month | Tickets, SLA, knowledge base |
| Tasks | $0.013698630136986/hour -- about $10.00/month | $0.05 per active task, above your first 25 (always free) | Projects, tasks, boards, timelines -- projects themselves are free |
| API Access | $0.013698630136986/hour -- about $10.00/month | $0.10 per 1,000 requests (about 50,000 free a month) · data transfer at $0.20 per GB (about 1 GB free a month) · $1.00 per active token per month, above your first 5 (always free) | Access tokens for the REST API. (Connecting an AI assistant is separate and free.) |

Contacts are shared by every workspace product (Marketing, Helpdesk, and your site forms all use the same contact database), so they are billed once for the whole workspace: **$5 per 1,000 contacts per month, with your first 500 free.**

**Automation emails and API data transfer.** Emails sent by your marketing automations count toward your email usage. They are billed at the same price as campaign emails, and they use the same free emails. The **Usage** page counts them in **Marketing Emails Sent**. API data transfer costs $0.20 per GB, and about 1 GB a month is free.

**Free emails, tickets, and API requests/data transfer build up hour by hour while their product is on, rather than being handed out all at once at the start of the month.** Marketing, for example, earns about 13.7 free emails for every hour it's on. A product earns no free allowance while it's off, so the exact amount you get follows the length of the month: a 30-day month earns a little less than the "about 10,000" figure, a 31-day month a little more, and February a little less still. Your 10 free GB of website bandwidth build up the same way, but all month, because that meter has no product to turn off. (Active tasks and API tokens are different -- their free amount is always the same fixed number, not something that builds up over time; see "How Usage Is Measured" below.) While the current month is still in progress, your bill and Usage page show this free amount as an estimate -- **Earned so far** plus what you'll earn by month end if the product stays on -- and it's only final once the month closes on the 1st.

> **Tip:** Module fees are the same every month regardless of how much you use the module. If the Articles module is enabled, it costs about $5.00/month whether you have 2 articles or 200.

### A Note About Article and Event Content

The Articles and Events module fees cover access to the feature itself. The actual content you create (each blog post, each event listing) counts as a page and is billed at the per-page rate. Turning either module on also raises your site's free page allowance by 10 (see [Pages](#pages) above), so the module's own content is often covered by the extra free pages it brings.

**Example:** You enable the Articles module ($5/month), which raises your site's free pages from 5 to 15, and create 10 blog posts. Since 10 pages fit inside your new 15-page allowance, you are charged just the $5 module fee -- no per-page charges, as long as you don't have other pages pushing you over 15.

## How Billing Works

### Monthly Billing Cycle

Your bill covers one calendar month. Bills are generated on the 1st of the month and are due by the 15th.

- Bills are paid through your WebNesting account using a credit or debit card.

### Pro-Rated Charges

You are never charged for time you did not use. If you enable or disable a feature partway through the month, your bill reflects only the time it was active.

**Example:** If you enable the Articles module on the 15th of a 30-day month, you are charged for roughly half the month -- about $2.50 instead of the full $5.

If you turn a site module off, its charge stops right away, any extra free pages it adds stop counting, and its content is **permanently deleted after 30 days** unless you turn it back on before then. Pages, storage and contacts are measured by the hour, so pages you add partway through the month are charged from the time they exist.

A workspace product (Marketing, Helpdesk, Tasks, API Access) gives you a choice when you turn it off: right away, or scheduled for the end of the billing month, which keeps it working -- and keeps its free allowance building up -- until then. See [Workspace Products](workspace-products.md#disabling-a-product) for the full turn-off flow.

### Small Balances Carry Forward

If a month's total comes to more than $0 but less than $0.50, it isn't billed on its own. Instead, it's added as a line item on your next month's invoice, labeled "Balance carried from {month}." You'll still see it and pay it -- it just travels with the following month's bill rather than being charged by itself. It is always on that bill when the bill is first emailed to you. If the following month's bill has already been sent, the balance goes on the next bill that hasn't been.

### Payment Processing

When you pay your bill, a small payment processing fee is added to cover credit card transaction costs. This fee is calculated automatically and shown on your bill before you confirm payment.

### Spending Alerts

The workspace owner gets an email the first time in a month that the month's charges pass a set amount. The amount depends on your account:

| Your account | Email when charges pass |
|---|---|
| New, with no payment method saved | $50 |
| A card saved | $150 |
| Paid an invoice of $250 or more, with no failed payment, refund or dispute in the last year | $500 |
| The same, with autopay on | $1,000 |

The charges are the ones your **Billing** page shows for the month, after your free allowance, credits and discounts and before the card fee. The email names the largest charge. When that is website bandwidth, it also gives the GB your sites sent and a tip: embedding long videos from YouTube or Vimeo costs nothing. A new workspace with no payment method is asked to add one, because WebNesting reviews such a workspace and may pause its sites. Every other account is told there is nothing to do. You get the email at most once a month for each amount, in the language you chose for WebNesting.

**Website bandwidth emails.** Website bandwidth has its own emails. Bandwidth updates once a day. After an update, the workspace owner gets an email the first time in the month that the website bandwidth charge passes $10, then $50, then $250, then $500, $1,000 and each doubling after that. The charge counts after your free 10 GB (less in the month your workspace was created). The email gives the GB your sites sent so far, the charge, the three files that sent the most and the next amount we email you at, with a **See Your Usage** button. If one update passes two amounts, you get one email naming the higher one. If the spending alert above goes out after the same update, the bandwidth email waits for the next update, so you get one email at a time.

### Late Payments

If a bill is not paid by its due date (the 15th), it is automatically marked overdue. You get an email reminder and a one-time 5% late fee is added to the outstanding balance.

The simplest way to never think about this is autopay: turn it on under **Billing → Settings** and your bill is paid automatically each month, so late fees never apply to you.

### Paused sites and videos

WebNesting can pause part of your workspace for billing. The **Billing** page always says what is paused and how to turn it back on.

- **Video playback, for an overdue invoice.** If an invoice stays unpaid, WebNesting can pause the videos you uploaded, so visitors to your sites cannot play them. Your pages still show. WebNesting can also stop changes in your dashboard until you pay, but the Billing page always lets you pay. The Billing page says "Video playback on your sites is paused until the overdue invoice is paid." Pay the invoice on the Billing page, and the videos come back within a few minutes. In **Files**, each paused video shows a pause badge. A video you add while playback is paused is paused too, and comes back with the others.
- **Your sites, for a review.** WebNesting reviews some new workspaces that have no payment method yet. During a review, WebNesting can pause all your sites. Visitors then see a "Temporarily unavailable" page in place of your site, and your videos are paused too. The workspace owner gets an email that says so. To turn your sites back on, add a payment method with the **Add Payment Method** button on the Billing page, or pay any overdue invoice. Your sites come back within a few minutes.
- **Your videos only, for a review.** WebNesting can also pause only your videos during a review, and keep your sites on. The Billing page then says "Video playback on your sites is paused while we review this workspace." Add a payment method with **Add Payment Method**, and the videos come back within a few minutes.

A pause deletes nothing. During a review you can still sign in and work in your dashboard.

## Viewing Your Billing

To see your current and past bills:

1. Open the workspace you want to look at.
2. Click **Settings** at the bottom of the left icon rail, then **Plans & Billing** under **Billing**. (You can also reach it from the **⋮** menu in the top bar → **Billing**.)
3. You will see your billing summary. Anything outstanding appears in a banner at the top -- you can pay a single invoice on the spot, or settle every unpaid invoice at once with one card payment and one receipt.
4. Each invoice groups its charges by your workspace and by each of your websites, with a last group, **From WebNesting**, for anything WebNesting gave you -- collapsed to one line with a total for each. Click a group's name to open just that group, or **Show itemized** to open them all -- each charge is listed underneath with its price and free allowance. Underneath, **Charges** adds up those amounts (each already has its free amount taken off), then the **Processing fee**, then your **Total**, and a **Refunded** amount if any of this invoice has been refunded.
5. **Click any line to see exactly how it was worked out.** A breakdown opens showing the math behind the amount (for example, "40 tickets − 20 free earned × $0.05 = $1.00"), a strip showing when the product was on this month, and -- for anything metered by events, like emails or tickets -- a chart of your usage against your free allowance with a projection to the end of the month.

If you have no unpaid bills, you will see an estimate of your current month's charges so far -- marked **Estimated Total** rather than a final number, since free amounts aren't final until the month closes on the 1st. Click any charge to see its projection to the end of the month.

### Credits, discounts and refunds

Anything WebNesting gives your workspace shows on your invoice -- never mixed silently into another amount. Most of it sits in one group at the end of the invoice, **From WebNesting**, one line for each thing, in the order the bill takes them: your price, complimentary, discounts and promotions, product credits, account credits, then credits for an earlier month. The detail under each line starts with the reason WebNesting gave.

- **Your price** (for example, "Your price: Marketing Emails Sent") -- WebNesting has set a price for your workspace that is lower than the list price. The charge itself is still shown at the list price, and this line takes off the difference. The line names both prices and the amount it covers, for example "$0.50 per 1,000 in place of $0.70 per 1,000 emails · 7,205 emails". It only applies to what you still owe after your free allowance and any bonus units, and it is taken off before any discount or credit. See [Your price](#your-price).
- **Complimentary** ("Complimentary Marketing" or "Complimentary account") -- WebNesting has made one product, or your whole account, free for a period. See [Complimentary](#complimentary).
- **A discount** (for example, "20% off Marketing", "20% off the Marketing fee", "$5.00 off Marketing Emails Sent" or "10% off your bill") -- a percentage or dollar discount on one product, one part of a product, or your whole bill. The line under it says what the percentage was taken of, for example "20% of the $18.36 Marketing owes". A percentage is worked out on what that product still owes after your free allowance, bonus units and your price, not on its full price. For example, if Marketing comes to $25.26 and your free emails cover $6.90 of it, a 20% discount is 20% of the remaining $18.36, which is $3.67. A discount never takes more than its own product owes.
- **A promotion** (for example, "Promotion: 20% off Marketing") -- an offer from WebNesting. If you entered a code for it, the line names the code. A percentage offer is worked out the same way as a discount. If two offers cover the same product, WebNesting may apply only the larger one. The other one still shows, at $0.00, with "Not applied: a larger discount on this product won." An offer can also give you bonus units or a credit instead of a discount; you see those the same way as any other bonus or credit.
- **A credit** ("Marketing credit" or "Account credit") -- dollars WebNesting gave you. A product credit is used only on that product's charges. An account credit is used for whatever the whole bill still owes, after everything else. The line under it says what this bill used, what is left and when it ends, for example "$14.90 used, $5.10 left, carries over until Dec 31".
- **Refund from WebNesting** -- a refund that support gave you as credit. See [How a refund reaches you](#how-a-refund-reaches-you).
- **Credit for a month** (for example, "Credit for August: 3,000 bonus emails") -- WebNesting gave you something for a month that was already billed. See [Credit for an earlier month](#credit-for-an-earlier-month).

**Bonus units** are not in that group. They show right under the charge they cover, as their own line, for example **Bonus emails from WebNesting**. Its amount is in units, not dollars, and the line under it says how many you earned and used this month. The charge above it says how many of its units were bonus units, for example "4,932 bonus". See [Bonus units](#bonus-units).

Two more lines you may see:

- **Late fee waived** -- shown struck through against the late fee it cancels, if support waives one for you.
- **Refunded** -- shown on the invoice's summary, and the invoice's status changes to **Refunded** once the full amount has been returned. A partial refund keeps the invoice's normal status and still shows the amount refunded.

Older invoices, and an account credit you had before October 2026, can show the older names "Account credit applied", "Promotional discount", "Discount" or a product name followed by "discount" (for example, "Helpdesk discount"). They mean the same things.

None of this ever changes your product prices or free allowances -- whatever WebNesting gives you is always a separate line, never a change to what things cost.

### Bonus units

Bonus units are extra units WebNesting gives your workspace on top of your free allowance -- for example 5,000 bonus emails a month. They are used after your free units, so you are charged only for what is left after both.

- **Every month.** Monthly bonus units build up hour by hour while their product is on, the same way your free allowance does. For example, 5,000 bonus emails a month earn about 6.8 emails an hour while Marketing is on, and none while it is off. Unused ones either end with each month or carry over until the bonus ends; the email you get says which.
- **All at once.** A one-time bonus is given in full the day it starts. It is used month by month until it runs out or ends.
- **While a product is off.** Bonus units for Marketing, Helpdesk, Tasks or API Access are not used, and do not build up, while that product is off. What you have left waits for you and still ends on its end date.
- **Things you have a number of.** A bonus on pages, contacts, storage, active tasks or API tokens is a fixed extra amount every month, on top of your free amount. It always ends with each month.
- **Complimentary products.** If a product is complimentary, its bonus units are not needed and are not spent. Bonus units that carry over stay yours for a later month.

You see your bonus units on the **Usage** page (see [What WebNesting gave you](#what-webnesting-gave-you)) and on your invoice, under the charge they cover. WebNesting emails you when a bonus is given, and again 7 days before it ends.

### Refund policy

**What is refunded.** WebNesting refunds a charge it billed in error in full. An error is a bug, a duplicate charge or a wrong usage figure. You choose whether the refund goes back to your card or comes as a credit on your next bill.

**What is not refunded.** Usage that was billed correctly is not refundable. WebNesting bills each month's usage after you have used it.

**How long you have.** Ask within 30 days of the invoice date.

**How to ask.** Open a Billing ticket with Support. WebNesting approves each refund, and Support tells you the result. If the refund is approved, see [How a refund reaches you](#how-a-refund-reaches-you).

### How a refund reaches you

Support can give a refund in three ways. Support tells you which one it used.

- **Back to your card.** The money returns to the card that paid the invoice. Your bank can take a few days to show it.
- **As account credit.** The amount is kept on your account and comes off your next bill. The line reads "Refund from WebNesting: account credit". It is used for whatever that bill still owes, and any part not used stays for the bill after it.
- **As credit for one product.** The amount comes off that product's charges on your next bill. The line names the product, for example "Refund from WebNesting: Marketing credit".

A refund can also be for a number of units from an earlier month, for example 3,000 emails from September. Those units are refunded at the price you paid for them that month, even if the price has changed since. If a discount or a special price lowered what you paid, the refund uses that lower price. If the refund is given as credit, the line on your next bill names the units and the month: "Refund from WebNesting: 3,000 September emails".

The earlier invoice itself does not change. Its summary shows the amount under **Refunded**, whichever way the refund was given. A refund can never be more than what the invoice came to.

Your activity log records each refund, for example "Refund issued: $10.00 as Marketing credit".

### Credit for an earlier month

Sometimes WebNesting gives you something that starts in the past: bonus units, a lower price, a credit, or a complimentary period. A month that is already billed does not change. You get the difference back as a credit on your next bill instead.

- The line starts with "Credit for" and the month, for example "Credit for August: 3,000 bonus emails", "Credit for August: your price for Marketing Emails Sent" or "Credit for August: complimentary Marketing".
- The line under it says how the amount was worked out, for example "3,000 emails at the August price of $0.70 per 1,000". The price is the one you paid that month, even if the price has changed since.
- You never get back more than you paid for those charges that month.
- The credit comes off what your next bill still owes, after everything else. If that bill owes less than the credit, the rest comes off the bill after it.
- If the earlier invoice is not paid yet, you still pay it in full. It shows nothing as refunded. The credit shows up on the first bill after you pay it.
- Once the earlier invoice is paid, it shows the same amount as refunded.
- The workspace owner gets one email for each earlier month. A price that comes with a bigger allowance is one email for that month, naming both credits, for example "They come back on your next invoice as a credit of **$2.43** for your price and **$3.50** for your allowance." Your invoice still shows them as two lines, such as "Credit for August: 5,000 emails from your allowance".

### Promotion codes

A promotion code is a code from WebNesting that gives your workspace an offer: a discount, bonus units or a credit.

To use a code on a workspace you already have:

1. Open the workspace, then **Settings** → **Plans & Billing**.
2. On the **Current Period** tab, find **Promotion code** under your charges and click **Have a code?**
3. Type the code and click **Check code**. You see what the code gives you, for example "20% off Marketing until Dec 31" or "5,000 bonus Marketing emails a month for 3 months". Nothing is applied yet.
4. Click **Apply to this workspace**. To try another code, click **Use a different code**.

After you apply it, the offer shows on your invoice as its own line, or as a bonus or credit like any other. If a larger discount on the same product already applies to your workspace, the page says so when you check the code: the code's line takes $0.00 off while the larger one lasts. A discount is taken off what the product still owes, so a product that already costs nothing that month shows no discount line.

You can also enter a code when you sign up or when you create a workspace. See [Creating Your Account](../account/creating-your-account.md).

If a code is refused, the page says why:

- **"That code is not valid. Check the spelling and try again."** The code does not exist, or it is for a different place. A sign-up code works only on the sign-up page.
- **"… ended on …"** The offer or the code is over.
- **"… is already applied to this workspace. A code works once for each workspace."** You already used it here.
- **"This code has been fully used."** The offer had a limited number of uses and they are gone.
- **"This code isn't available for this workspace."** The offer is for other workspaces, for example new ones only.
- **"Too many tries. You can try again in … minutes."** After five wrong codes in 10 minutes, code entry is locked for a short time. Wait the number of minutes the message names, then try again.

You need permission to manage billing in the workspace to see the **Promotion code** section.

### Your price

If WebNesting has agreed a price with you that is lower than the list price, that price is shown wherever you see what something costs, with the label **Your price** and the list price beside it. You will see it on the **Products** page, in the **Cost calculator**, on the add-ons under **API Access**, and when you turn on a product or a site module. The **Usage** page marks the same item with **Your price**, without an amount, because that page shows counts and not money. A price can be for one website or for every website in your workspace, and it can have an end date. After the end date the list price applies again.

On your invoice the charge itself stays at the list price, and a separate **Your price** line takes off the difference (see above). The two together are what you pay.

A price can come with a bigger free allowance, for example 5,000 more free emails a month. You earn it by the hour while the product is on, like your normal free allowance, and unused units end with each month. It has one name everywhere: "Your allowance", for example "Your allowance: 5,000 emails a month".

- The **Usage** page lists it with that name.
- Your invoice shows it as its own line with that name, right under the charge it covers. Its amount is in units, like a bonus line. The charge above it does not count these units as bonus.
- The **You have a special price for …** email names it, so you get one email for the price and the allowance together.
- If you turn the product off, the free units you give up include your allowance.
- The **Products** page and the **Cost calculator** count your allowance in the free amount, as your invoice does.

### Complimentary

WebNesting can make one product, or your whole account, complimentary for a period. Complimentary means you are not charged for it.

Your invoice still lists every charge, so you can see what you used. One line then takes those charges off:

- **Complimentary** followed by the product's name (for example, "Complimentary Marketing") covers every charge for that product. Everything else on the invoice is billed as normal.
- **Complimentary account** covers every charge on the invoice. The invoice closes at $0.00, nothing is charged to your card, and no invoice email is sent for it. You can still open the invoice under **Invoice History** on your Billing page. It says "This invoice closed at $0.00. No invoice email was sent for it." above its charges.

The line shows the reason WebNesting gave, and what it covers. If the complimentary period starts or ends partway through a month, the line covers only the usage inside that period and says which dates, for example "covers every Marketing charge from Sep 16". The usage outside those dates is billed as normal. For a complimentary account, only a month it covers from the first day to the last closes at $0.00. A month it covers only in part is billed, and its invoice email is sent, as usual.

A complimentary line takes off what you would have owed after your free allowance. Bonus units, credits and discounts you also have are not spent on charges that are complimentary. A credit keeps its balance, and bonus units that carry over stay yours for a later month.

### Estimating Costs Before You Add Something

The **Cost calculator** tab on the Billing page starts from what's already on in your account -- your enabled products, sites, and modules, using your actual usage as a starting point -- and lets you add or remove anything to see the difference: another product, an add-on, a second website, more pages, more contacts. Every line shows its own monthly cost and the math behind it, with an **Estimated per month** total at the bottom. **Start Over from My Account** resets it back to what you actually have on today.

## Understanding Your Usage

Your billing page and usage data help you understand exactly what you are paying for.

### What Counts as Usage

Here is a quick summary of what is tracked:

| Resource | How It Is Counted |
|----------|-------------------|
| Pages | Total number of pages on your site at any given time |
| Storage | Everything we store for you across your workspace -- your uploads, helpdesk attachments, and form uploads |
| Website bandwidth | The data your public websites send to visitors, in GB for the whole workspace, added up over the month |
| Admin users | Number of people with admin access to your site |
| Modules | Whether each module is turned on or off |

### How Usage Is Measured

Not everything is measured the same way -- it depends on whether what's being counted is a **level** (how many you have right now) or an **event** (something that happened once).

**Pages, storage, and contacts are levels.** WebNesting tracks your usage continuously throughout the month. If your usage changes (for example, you add 10 new pages, then delete 3), your bill reflects the actual usage over time. You are not charged a flat rate for your peak usage -- it is averaged over the billing period.

**Example:** If you had 20 pages for the first half of the month and then added 10 more for the second half, your bill would reflect something between 20 and 30 pages, not the full 30 for the entire month.

**Emails, tickets, API calls and website bandwidth are events.** Each email you send, ticket you create, API call you make, or GB your websites send is counted and charged exactly once, at the moment it happens -- never averaged, never charged twice. This holds even if you turn the product off later that same month: an email you already sent stays billed at the same per-email price whether or not Marketing is still on by the time the month ends. The one exception is tickets: a ticket counts only while it exists, so deleting one during the month takes it off that month's bill. Once a month has closed, its bill no longer changes.

## Tips for Managing Costs

Here are some practical ways to keep your WebNesting bill manageable.

### Take Advantage of Free Allowances

Remember that your first 5 pages (more if Articles, Events, or Store is turned on) and first 2 GB of storage are free, and letting people sign up on your site costs nothing extra. For a simple website, you may not owe anything beyond the modules you choose to enable.

### Only Enable Modules You Need

Each enabled module adds to your monthly bill. If you are not actively using a module, disable it.

> **Important:** Disabling a module permanently deletes its content -- blog posts, events, products -- after 30 days, or right away if you choose **Delete now**. Turn the module back on before then to keep it. See [Modules and Features](modules-and-features.md#how-to-disable-a-module).

### Keep Website Bandwidth Low

Your first 10 GB each month are free. To stay under it, put long videos on YouTube or Vimeo instead of uploading them. Check **Files that sent the most** on the **Usage** page now and then. See [Website bandwidth](#website-bandwidth).

### Manage Your Files

Storage can add up if you upload large files. To keep your storage costs down:

- Delete images and files you are no longer using on your site. Click **Files** in the top bar to browse everything your workspace stores.
- Resize large images before uploading. Photos straight from a camera can be 5-10 MB each, but a web-optimized version at 200-500 KB looks just as good on screen.
- Remove duplicate files.

### Review Your Pages

If you have pages that are no longer needed (old test pages, outdated content, duplicate pages), consider deleting them to reduce your page count.

### Check Your Bill Regularly

Get in the habit of reviewing your billing page once a month. This helps you catch anything unexpected early and make adjustments if needed.
